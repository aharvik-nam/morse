MORSE TRAINER

Settings

Chart

Tree is the easiest to read: from “start”, every dot takes you one step down to the left and every dash one step down to the right. Card shows the same paths packed together like a pocket reference card: round pads are dots, bars are dashes.

Key Free mode

Buttons: one press of ← is one dot, one press of → is one dash. The simplest way to start. Paddle uses the same two buttons, but keeps the rhythm for you like a real electronic keyer: hold a button and it repeats, hold both and it alternates dot–dash. Straight key is the classic single telegraph key (Space): a short press is a dot and a long press is a dash.

In Learn mode the level decides the key type and the speed, so these two settings only apply to Free mode.

Paddle speed Free mode

How fast the paddle sends, in words per minute (WPM). It sets the length of a dot; a dash is three dots long. A pause of three dots ends a letter and seven ends a word.

Sound

Plays the tone for every dot and dash, so you learn the sound together with the path.

How it works

Morse code in one minute

Every letter is a short pattern of two sounds: a dot (short) and a dash (long, three times as long as a dot). E is one dot, T is one dash, A is dot–dash.

The chart

The chart is a map of all the patterns. Begin at start for every letter. Each dot moves the light one step along a dotted line, each dash one step along a dashed line. The letter you land on is the letter you sent.

  • Red: where the light is now.
  • Yellow: the path you have taken, and in Learn mode the path you should take next.
  • Green: letters that are new on this level.

Click any letter to see and hear its pattern.

Learn mode

You spell a word one letter at a time. There is no time limit. A wrong press just starts that letter again.

  • Each level has six words, and a short note pops up to tell you what is new. Click the level name to read it again.
  • After six words the next level opens. You can also step between levels with ◀ ▶.

What makes it harder

Only one thing changes at a time:

  • More characters. Letters come a few at a time, shortest patterns first. Numbers and punctuation come last.
  • Less help on the chart. First a yellow path shows the whole way. Then only the letter is marked. Then nothing is marked. A wrong turn always brings one step of help back for that letter.
  • The key. You start with simple buttons. Later they become a paddle that repeats while you hold, and it gets faster.
  • The words. A letter costs one point per dot or dash, plus one point every time the pattern switches between dot and dash. A word is the sum of its letters, and within a level you get the low-scoring words first.

Free mode

Send whatever you like. Pause after a letter and it is written in the text box; a longer pause adds a space. Numbers and Punctuation add those characters to the chart.

Fixing a mistake

In Free mode, send seven or more dots in a row. That is the Morse “error” sign, and it deletes the last word.
